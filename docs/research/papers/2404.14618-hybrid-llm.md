## What the paper reports

The arXiv abstract says the method makes up to 40% fewer calls to the large model with no drop in response quality. The sections read for this summary describe a two-model router whose test-time knob is a threshold on a predicted score, not a live price.

## Method

An "easy" query is one where the small model's response quality is close to the large model's, not necessarily a query that is cheap to answer. The quality gap is the small model's quality minus the large model's quality, treated as random because decoding is random. Quality in the experiments is the BART score.

A BERT-style encoder (DeBERTa-v3-large, 300M parameters, in the reported setup) reads the query once and emits a score in [0, 1]. Queries above a threshold go to the small model. The paper treats the router's own cost as negligible next to autoregressive decoding.

Three training targets, all binary cross-entropy on MixInstruct:

- **Deterministic.** One sampled response from each model. The label is whether the small model's BART score is at least the large model's.
- **Probabilistic.** The label is the fraction of 10 paired samples in which the small model matches or beats the large model.
- **Transformed probabilistic.** When the large model wins almost everywhere, those labels are nearly all zero (the paper says 0 for almost 90% of queries on FLAN-T5 800M versus Llama-2 13B). The target is relaxed to "small model is within t of the large model." t is chosen by grid search to spread the labels apart.

The threshold is chosen on a calibration slice, not learned as a dollar weight. Section 4.5 draws 500 validation examples and searches for the threshold with the largest share of small-model calls whose BART-score drop stays within 1%.

Data: 10,000 MixInstruct training instructions, 10 responses per model, and the dataset's own 5,000-example validation and test splits. Model pairs are drawn from FLAN-T5 (800M and 11B), Llama-2 (7B and 13B), and GPT-3.5-turbo. Training is 5 epochs on one A100 80GB. Code is linked from the paper at `https://github.com/m365-core/hybrid_llm_routing`.

## Key results

From §4.2 and §4.4–4.5, as stated in the prose:

- Small gap (Llama-2 7B versus 13B). The deterministic router sends 20% of queries to the small model with a 0.1% quality drop against always-large, and 40% with a 0.2% drop. The probabilistic and transformed routers reach a 20% cost advantage with no quality drop.
- Medium gap (Llama-2 13B versus GPT-3.5-turbo). All three routers reach a 20% cost advantage with at most a 1% quality drop, and a 40% cost advantage with at most a 4% drop.
- Large gap (FLAN-T5 800M versus Llama-2 13B). The deterministic and probabilistic routers are only marginally better than random. The transformed router reaches a 40% cost advantage with a 10.3% quality drop, 3.5 and 2.8 percentage points lower than the other two routers.
- At a modest 10% cost advantage on the small-gap pair, the probabilistic router can beat always-large, because the small model sometimes scores higher.
- Router latency, averaged over 200 random queries, is about 10 times lower than the fastest LLM they timed (FLAN-T5 800M). The three routers share one encoder, so they share that latency.
- Thresholds picked on the 500-example slice carry over to the test split. On the Llama-2 7B/13B pair the selected thresholds give a cost advantage above 96%, with a small extra quality drop from validation to test.

Cost in this paper is the fraction of queries sent to the large model. The decision does not multiply a token estimate by a vendor price.
