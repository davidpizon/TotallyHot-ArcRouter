## Abstract

> Recent works show that assembling multiple off-the-shelf large language models (LLMs) can harness their complementary abilities. To achieve this, routing is a promising method, which learns a router to select the most suitable LLM for each query. However, existing routing models are ineffective when multiple LLMs perform well for a query. To address this problem, in this paper, we propose a method called query-based Router by Dual Contrastive learning (RouterDC). The RouterDC model consists of an encoder and LLM embeddings, and we propose two contrastive learning losses to train the RouterDC model. Experimental results show that RouterDC is effective in assembling LLMs and largely outperforms individual top-performing LLMs as well as existing routing methods on both in-distribution (+2.76%) and out-of-distribution (+1.90%) tasks. Source code is available at https://github.com/shuhao02/RouterDC .

## Method

The router is an encoder plus one learned embedding per LLM. Selection probabilities are a softmax over cosine similarities between the query embedding and those LLM embeddings. The encoder in the experiments is mDeBERTaV3-base (86M parameters). LLM embeddings are 768-dimensional. The whole router is under 100M parameters, and training does not backpropagate into the candidate LLMs.

Scores that supervise the router come from the candidates' own outputs. On open-ended items the LLM is sampled M times (M = 10, beam sampling, temperature 0.2) and the score is the fraction of answers marked correct. On multiple-choice items the score is a normalized probability of the correct option, or 0 if the greedy answer is wrong.

Two losses:

- **Sample-LLM.** For each query, the top K+ scoring LLMs are positives and the bottom K− LLMs that scored under 0.5 are negatives (K+ = K− = 3). The query embedding is pulled toward the positive LLM embeddings and pushed from the negative ones. K+ greater than 1 is the point of the paper: several LLMs can be acceptable, so a KL match to a softmax over scores (the ZOOTER objective they criticize) is the wrong target.
- **Sample-sample.** Training queries are embedded with the pretrained encoder, reduced with t-SNE, and clustered with k-means (N = 5 groups). A query is pulled toward another query in its group and pushed from H queries outside it (H = 3). This is there because sample-LLM training alone was unstable: similar queries could land far apart and be routed differently.

The training objective adds the two losses with a weight λ, set to 1. Inference picks the LLM with the largest cosine similarity and calls only that LLM.

Candidates are seven 7B–8B models (Mistral-7B and two Llama-3 variants, plus four Mistral fine-tunes). In-distribution tasks are MMLU, GSM8K, CMMLU, ARC-Challenge, and HumanEval, with non-default splits at 70/30 and the training portions pooled. Out-of-distribution tasks are PreAlgebra, MBPP, and C-Eval. Training is 1,000 AdamW steps, learning rate 5×10⁻⁵, weight decay 0.01, batch size 64, on A100 80GB GPUs. The paper says that run took 28.3 minutes.

## Key results

- Abstract: +2.76% in distribution and +1.90% out of distribution against existing routing methods.
- Section 4.2 prose: on the five in-distribution tasks, average accuracy beats the best single LLM (dolphin-2.9-llama3-8b) by 3.98%, with per-task gains of +0.51% (GSM8K), +0.57% (ARC-Challenge), and +1.63% (HumanEval) over that model's own scores. Average accuracy is +2.77% over LoraRetriever. Inference is about 6× faster than voting, which calls every LLM.
- Out of distribution, average accuracy is 1.9% above the same best single LLM. Per-task scores are close to that LLM rather than far above it (38.81 versus 39.72 on PreAlgebra, 46.80 versus 47.34 on MBPP, 51.93 versus 52.01 on C-Eval). Among the routing baselines, only RouterDC exceeds that LLM on average.
- Sensitivity: λ in [0.5, 5], cluster counts in [4, 9], and H ≥ 1 change average accuracy little. Using only the single best LLM as the positive (K+ = 1, K− = 6) is the worst of the K settings they plot, and still beats the best single LLM's 54.56% in-distribution average.

Appendix I is the only cost experiment. On GSM8K and MBPP from RouterBench, they replace the training score s with s + c, where c is the cost of that query on that LLM, and report that RouterDC is more cost-effective than CosineClassifier and ZOOTER. The main method and the inference rule do not take a price. The appendix does not describe a live price or a separation between token count and unit price.
